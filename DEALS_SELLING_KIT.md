# DEALS SELLING KIT

Built 2026-09-03 from `OPS_BOARD.md`, `SUBCONTRACT_APPROACH_PACK.md`,
`AU_PANEL_CHECKLIST.md`, `DIRECT_INCOME_ROUTES.md`. This is the material
you put in front of a buyer — capability statement, one-pager, outreach
messages, pricing skeleton, objection answers, scoping checklist.

**Every section below is DRAFT — OPERATOR MUST REVIEW AND EDIT BEFORE
USE.** No certification, client, project, revenue figure, years of
experience, insurance policy, or qualification has been invented
anywhere in this file. Every place a personal fact belongs is a
bracketed placeholder. Fill only what is true. Leave a placeholder
visible rather than guess — a blank is honest, a guess is not.

**One open legal question before you sell anything under the name
"penetration testing" specifically:** `DIRECT_INCOME_ROUTES.md` found no
Commonwealth licensing regime, and structurally the Queensland *Security
Providers Act 1993* looks like it covers physical security, not IT
security testing — but this was not confirmed against the current
in-force text. Get the Queensland Office of Fair Trading's answer in
writing before selling formal penetration testing under that name.
General security review, hardening, incident cleanup, and awareness
training are not affected by this question.

## 5. OBJECTION-HANDLING PAGE (the most valuable page in this kit)

**DRAFT — OPERATOR MUST REVIEW AND EDIT BEFORE USE.** These answers are
written to be truthful under pressure. Do not soften them into implying
something you don't have — a buyer who later discovers a gap you glossed
over will not give you a second engagement. Practise saying these out
loud until they feel natural, not defensive.

> ---
> ### "Do you have insurance?"
>
> **Honest answer:** "Not at the moment — I'm a sole trader and haven't
> taken out a professional indemnity or public liability policy yet.
> If this engagement needs it, I can get a quote and have a policy in
> place before we start — sole-trader PI typically runs from around
> $40-80/month, so it's not a long lead time. What level does this
> engagement need?"
>
> Do not say you have insurance if you don't, do not imply a quote in
> progress is a policy in place, and do not agree to start
> insurance-dependent work before the policy is actually bound.
>
> ### "Who else have you worked with? Can I speak to a reference?"
>
> **Honest answer:** "I don't have corporate client references yet — I'm
> early in building that track record. What I can show you instead is
> [YOUR RELEVANT EXPERIENCE — FILL IN: concrete, checkable work — a
> public profile, a disclosed finding, a codebase you can walk through
> live, a completed project]. I'd also suggest starting with a small,
> low-risk piece of work — that becomes your own direct reference for
> whether I'm worth trusting with something bigger."
>
> Never invent a client or project to fill this gap. A checkable
> substitute (public profile, live demo, disclosed finding) is stronger
> than a name you can't back up if asked.
>
> ### "Are you certified? (OSCP/CREST/CISSP/etc.)"
>
> **Honest answer:** "No, not currently. I've built my capability through
> [YOUR RELEVANT EXPERIENCE — FILL IN — hands-on practice, disclosed
> work, self-directed study] rather than a formal cert path so far. If a
> named certification is a hard requirement for this specific
> engagement — some government and regulated-industry work does require
> one by policy — then I'm honestly not the right fit for that

> started. For work where the requirement is 'can this person actually
> find and fix the problem,' I'm happy to be judged on a practical test
> or a small paid task instead of a certificate."
>
> ### "What if you get hit by a bus? Who covers this if something happens

>
> **Honest answer:** "That's a fair question for a one-person operation —
> there's no bench behind me. For anything time-critical or
> business-critical, that's worth factoring into how you scope the

## 6. "WHAT I NEED FROM YOU" — SCOPING CHECKLIST

**DRAFT — OPERATOR MUST REVIEW AND EDIT BEFORE USE.** Ask these before
quoting anything. Do not quote a fixed price against an unanswered
question in this list — that is how scope creep and unpaid extra work
happen.

> ---
> **Before I can quote, I need to know:**
>
> 1. **What's the actual problem or goal?** In your own words, not a
>    technical spec — what outcome are you trying to get to?
> 2. **What's in scope, specifically?** Which systems, sites, networks,
>    accounts, or code — named explicitly. Anything not named is out of
>    scope by default.
> 3. **Who owns authorisation to test/review this?** I need written
>    sign-off from someone with the actual authority to grant it before
>    any testing work starts — no exceptions, this protects both of us.
> 4. **Is there anything I must NOT touch or test?** Production systems,
>    live customer data, third-party-hosted components, anything with a
>    separate owner.
> 5. **What's the timeframe?** Hard deadline, or flexible? Any blackout
>    windows (e.g. no testing during business hours, no changes before a
>    launch)?
> 6. **What does "done" look like to you?** A written report? A fixed
>    list of issues resolved? A retest after fixes? Say this up front so
>    it's not renegotiated at the end.
> 7. **Has anyone else looked at this before?** Prior audits, known
>    issues already on your list, previous incidents — I don't want to
>    charge you to rediscover something you already know.
> 8. **What's your budget range, roughly?** Not to set the price
>    unfairly — to tell me quickly whether the scope you want matches
>    what's realistic to fund, before I spend time on a full quote.
> 9. **Who's my point of contact during the work?** One named person who
>    can answer questions and receive urgent findings immediately if
>    something serious turns up mid-engagement.
> 10. **Any compliance/regulatory constraints I should know about?**
>     Industry-specific rules, data residency, notification obligations
>     if something is found — better to know before starting than
>     discover mid-engagement.
> ---

## HOW TO USE THIS KIT TODAY

1. Fill Section 1 (capability statement) first — every other document
   in this kit borrows from it.
2. Fill Section 4 (pricing) deliberately, not by copying a number from
   anywhere else — no verified market rate exists for this exact
   position; decide yours.
3. Read Section 5 (objections) out loud twice before your first call —
   it is the difference between sounding defensive and sounding honest.
4. Pick the right outreach variant from Section 3 per target — see
   `SUBCONTRACT_APPROACH_PACK.md` for named firms already checked as
   having a real entry path, and `OPS_BOARD.md` for live tender/RFI
   targets Section 3c applies to.
5. Get the Queensland OFT's written answer on the pentesting-licensing
   question (see the note at the top of this file) before selling
   formal penetration testing under that name — everything else in
   this kit is unaffected by that question.
